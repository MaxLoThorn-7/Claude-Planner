Why it worked

Independent signals compound.

Each of those 5 votes came from someone who looked at the urn independently. No coordination. Each vote is real evidence.

This is the Condorcet Jury theorem: when voters are independent and each better than chance, majority vote is more accurate than any single voter. The math compounds in your favor.

The failure mode

When people stop looking.

Majority only works if the votes are independent. What if people copy each other instead of gathering real evidence?

Independent

5 people, 5 independent looks at the urn.

Cascade

Only A looked at the urn. B, C, D, E copied the person before them.

In a cascade, a 5–0 vote is not five independent signals. It's one signal, repeated four times. Majority still reads "overwhelming consensus." The evidence is much weaker than it looks.

Stock bubbles. Fashion trends. Viral opinions. All cascades — confidence without proportional evidence.

The real risk

Not laziness. Cascade.

The concern about AI isn't that it makes people lazy. It's a structural problem that looks exactly like a cascade.

The echo chamber

10 coworkers, same AI, same answer

Everyone asks the same question. They compare notes: "All 10 of us got the same recommendation!" They treat this as strong consensus from 10 independent minds.

It's one signal, echoed 10 times.

Using it as a tool

You ask AI to lay out the pros and cons. You read it, weigh it, push back on it, and form your own view. The AI informed your thinking.

You're the independent signal.

The rule

Stay a signal.

Use AI to understand things better. Stay the one doing the thinking.

The tool isn't the problem. Outsourcing your judgment is.

Next time you see strong consensus: are these independent signals, or is everyone copying the same source?
